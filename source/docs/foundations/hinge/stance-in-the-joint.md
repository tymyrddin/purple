# The stance in the joint

The position has an occupant, and under pressure the occupant takes a posture. Satir watched people communicate when
they felt threatened and described [four recurring ones](../organisational-development/satir-core.md). She read them as
ways of managing stress rather than as faults of character. Each one changes what the occupant can recognise from the
position.

Placating takes the other party's frame to keep the peace. The auditor's categories become the categories. No new
distinction gets made, because the distinctions on offer are all somebody else's, repeated back.

Blaming keeps the occupant's own frame. What arrives from outside reads as noise or as fault, so the
position stays exactly as wide as it was, and gets defended at that size.

Computing retreats into logic and procedure, leaving the emotional content of the situation outside. In security it
shows up as human problems answered with policies, and policy problems answered with more detailed policies.

Distracting leaves the position altogether. The subject changes, and the question of who is standing there stops being
asked.

Congruence is the alternative rather than a fifth stance. What is said lines up with what is sensed and with what the
situation requires, and it leaves the occupant able to notice their own width.

## The one that builds things

Three of the four read as avoidance on sight. Computing does not, because it produces. It writes the severity matrix,
the control catalogue, the architecture diagram, the maturity model, and the output is real work, frequently good work.

It also produces one particular kind of artefact: the outcome is supposed to follow from the structure, with nobody's
judgement anywhere in the path. A matrix that decides. A policy with no discretion left in it. That is not a coincidence
of style. A stance that has set the person aside will build things that have nowhere to put one.

Borrowing from mathematics is the same move at a higher pitch. Mathematics is where content lives without a self, so a
vocabulary taken from there arrives already stripped of its occupant. That is the attraction, and it is what the
[three cases](borrowed-precision.md) find wanting.

## The dissonance is the yield

A construction of that kind meets the situation and does not fit. The gap that opens is the one thing the position can
register from inside itself, and it registers in two vocabularies at once.
[Weinberg calls it an error](../system-effectiveness/core-triad.md), evidence that the model used to design the thing
does not match how the work happens. Satir calls it incongruence, the friction between what is being said and what is
being sensed. Same event, two readings, and neither treats it as an embarrassment.

The gap is where the position finds out its own shape. It is available nowhere else, because the model can only be
checked against the work by the person holding it. An occupant who has smoothed the gap away has removed the only
instrument in reach.

Sometimes what it finds is that a road ends. Of the three borrowings, one widens the position and two do not, and
knowing which is which is worth more than the vocabulary would have been. A dead end reported is a result. A dead end
walked twice is what not reporting it costs.

## Which one is holding the position

None of the four gets chosen once and for all, and none belongs to a person as a property. The stance shifts with the
pressure, fastest when the pressure is highest. That is also when the gap is most worth reading.

The useful question is not which stance somebody is. It is which one is holding the position at the moment the situation
declines to fit, and whether the occupant is still in a condition to notice.
