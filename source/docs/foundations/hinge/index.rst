Chasing the hinge
=============================================

.. image:: /_static/images/hinge.png
  :alt: A door hinge rendered as a technical illustration, but the central pin of the hinge is a human silhouette, standing upright, holding the two leaves together. The left leaf is dense and disordered: overlapping raw material, weather, cables, half-legible signals, textures that do not resolve. The right leaf is clean and ruled: a severity matrix grid, policy clauses, an architecture diagram with tidy boxes and arrows.

A control gets written so that the outcome follows from the wording. A severity matrix decides what an alert
is worth, a policy leaves no judgement to exercise, an architecture diagram fixes how the behaviour will go.
A system that works regardless of who runs it needs nobody to be trusted.

It never quite arrives. The matrix needs somebody to decide which field it reads, the policy needs somebody
to say what counts as an exception, and the diagram is
:doc:`a claim about the estate <../../systems-architecture/architecture-as-model>` made by whoever drew it.
Route around the person at one point and the requirement turns up at another.

Somebody stands between what gets encountered and the account made of it. That position is the hinge, the
joint both sides turn on, and it is no wider than its occupant can tell things apart.

Lewin's field theory says why the position is person-shaped: behaviour is a joint function of the person and
the situation as that person reads it, so
:doc:`a model never gets read off a system <../change-management/lewin>`. Ashby's variety
gives the width a size, and
:doc:`the count depends on who is doing the distinguishing <../system-effectiveness/ashby>`.

What stocks a position that size is mostly borrowed. Few distinctions get worked out from scratch, and the
ones picked up arrive with a vocabulary attached. Not all of it survives the journey. What the occupant can
do with any of it depends on the posture they are holding when the situation declines to fit.
